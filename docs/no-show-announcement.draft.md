## 1. To members — board post (type: Share, city-wide) or newsletter block

**Subject / title:** A small change so free events stop losing seats 🎟️

Hi everyone,

Most of our events are free, and most of them fill up. When someone books
a seat and doesn't come, that seat was somebody else's night out. So from
now on, free events keep a simple record of who showed up. Here is how it
works, in full, so nothing surprises you.

**What counts as a no-show**
You were confirmed for a *free* event, the host ran check-in at the door,
and you neither checked in nor cancelled at least **12 hours** before the
start. That's it. Paid events are never part of this.

**What happens**
- **First time in 90 days → a yellow card.** It's a heads-up, nothing more.
  You'll get an email and a notification, and the next time you RSVP we'll
  ask you to confirm you're really coming. It expires on its own.
- **Second time in 90 days → a red card.** RSVPs and waitlists pause for
  **30 days**. You have **48 hours** to appeal before the pause starts, and
  an admin reads every appeal.

**The fair-play rules built in**
- **Cancel in time and nothing happens.** Up to 12 hours before the start,
  cancelling is exactly what we want you to do — it hands the seat to the
  waitlist.
- **If you were there but the scan was missed**, tell the host. They can
  clear it in one click and it never counts.
- **We only count events where check-in really ran.** If the host scanned
  only a couple of people, nobody gets a card.
- **Nothing is public.** Cards are between you, the host of that event, and
  the admins. Other members never see them.

**One more thing: "still coming?"**
For free events with limited spots, you'll get a message the day before
asking if you're still coming. Tap yes and you're set. If you don't answer
and there's a waitlist, your seat is released 12 hours before the start —
before it would count as a no-show, so you're never penalised for silence,
you just lose the seat to someone waiting.

Cancel when plans change, check in when you arrive, and none of this will
ever touch you. Thanks for keeping our events full of people who actually
turn up.

— Nate

## 2. To hosts — host group / WhatsApp, or a note in the host panel

**Subject:** How no-show cards work on your events (and what you control)

Hosts,

Free events now settle no-shows automatically. Here's the whole system from
your side, because you're the only person who can clear a card.

**When cards are issued**
About **2 hours after your event ends**, if you ran check-in for at least
**half the room**, every confirmed attendee who neither checked in nor
cancelled 12 hours before the start gets a card. First one in 90 days is
yellow (a warning), second is red (RSVPs paused 30 days, after a 48-hour
appeal window). Paid events are never processed.

**Why the half-the-room rule matters**
If you only scanned a few people, the system assumes the door stopped
scanning, not that everyone else stayed home — and issues nothing. So:
**scan everyone who walks in.** Missed scans are the main way a good member
ends up with a card.

**What you see**
- A notification and an email when cards are issued for your event, with
  the count.
- A **No-shows tab** on your event's participants page listing every card
  from that event.
- On the **approval queue**, a small 🟨 next to anyone who currently holds an
  active card. It's a count, not a history, and it only appears while a card
  is active. Approved lists and waitlists show nothing.

**What you control**
- **Clear a card** from the No-shows tab. Use it whenever someone was there
  — arrived late, scan missed, you know they came. Say why (it goes in the
  audit log); the member is told the card no longer counts. A card cleared
  this way never counts toward a red.
- **Nothing else.** Cards are issued by the system, appeals go to admins.
  You never have to be the one who penalises anyone.

**The day-before reconfirmation**
Free events with limited spots ask attendees "still coming?" the day
before. Anyone who doesn't answer loses the seat to the waitlist 12 hours
before the start — but only if someone is actually waiting. Expect a few
seats to move overnight; that's the system working.

**What it asks of you**
1. Run check-in properly, every time, everyone who arrives.
2. If you finish scanning the morning after, that's fine — the sweep waits.
3. Clear cards for late arrivals and missed scans as soon as you notice.

Questions, or a card you think is wrong: message Nate.
