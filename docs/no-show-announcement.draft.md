## 1. To members — board post (type: Share, city-wide) or newsletter block

**Subject / title:** Keeping seats for the people who come 🎟️

Hi everyone,

A lot of our events are small: a dinner table, a boat, a room with twenty
chairs. When someone takes one of those seats and doesn't come, that was
somebody else's evening. So from **16 September**, Smileys keeps a simple
record of whether you turn up for the seats you take. We call it your
**standing**. Here's all of it, so nothing surprises you.

**It only concerns limited events**
That means any event with **limited spots**, whatever its size: a dinner
table, a boat, a Let's Get Social with a waitlist. It also covers a booking the
host has promised to a venue. Events with no cap on who comes never affect your
standing.

**What counts**
- **Not coming.** When the host marks you absent, or when the host checked
  people in and you weren't checked in. The host gets that list the next
  morning and can excuse you until the end of that day. If nobody was checked
  in at all, nothing counts against anyone: you can't get a mark because a
  host skipped the door.
- **Cancelling late.** Cancelling less than **24 hours** before a limited event
  starts counts the same as not coming, because the seat usually can't be
  filled by then. Two exceptions:
  - if someone from the waitlist takes your seat and comes, it doesn't count;
  - if you're answering the day-before "still coming?" message, it doesn't count.

A seat you took less than **3 hours** before the start never counts against
you either way: a last-minute waitlist claim isn't a seat anyone else lost.

Cancelling earlier than that is exactly what we want you to do. It hands the
seat to someone waiting.

**What happens**
- **Two of those within 90 days: a yellow card.** You'll get a notification.
  While you have it, you join limited waitlists at the back. It clears after
  **two events where you're checked in**. Any event counts, open ones included.
- **One more while on yellow: a red card.** The host approves your seat at
  limited events, and you join their waitlists at the back. After **three
  events where you're checked in**, an admin reviews your card and restores
  your standing.
- **Open events are never blocked.** Whatever your standing, you can always
  come to something, and showing up is how a card clears.
- A card with no RSVPs for **90 days** simply lapses.

**The fair-play parts**
- **Marked absent by mistake?** Go to **Your standing** and tap **"I was
  there"** within 30 days. A moderator checks with the host, and it's never the
  host of that event who decides. While it's being looked at, nothing new
  counts against you.
- **Get checked in when you arrive.** Being checked in is what clears a card,
  and it's the best proof you were there.
- **It's private.** Only you can see your standing. A host only ever sees a
  small 🟨 or 🟥 next to a request to join their event, and nothing else.
- **New cities get a head start.** For a city's first 90 days, nothing counts
  toward a card there.
- **Everyone starts clean.** Nothing from before 16 September counts, and the
  old no-show cards are gone.

**About "still coming?"**
For limited events with nothing to pay in advance, you'll get a message the
day before asking whether you're still coming. Tap yes and you're set. If you
don't answer and someone is waiting, your seat goes to them 12 hours before the
start. That never counts against you; you just lose the seat.

You can see your own record any time at **Your standing**. Cancel early when
plans change, get checked in when you arrive, and none of this will ever touch
you. Thanks for keeping our tables full of people who actually come.

— Nate

## 2. To hosts — host group / WhatsApp, or a note in the host panel

**Subject:** Check-in and standing: what's changed for your events

Hosts,

From 16 September, whether members turn up is recorded on **limited events**
(any event with limited spots, whatever its size, or one you mark as a promised
booking). It's called
standing. You don't hand out cards; the system does. What you do decide is
who came. Here's everything from your side.

**At the door**
- You'll get a **"Check-in is open"** notification around the start time, with
  a link straight to your roster.
- The roster lives at **Host panel → Check-In**. The event page also shows a
  "Check people in" card on the day, and the participants page has a Check-in
  button. Tap each person as they arrive. **QR scanning now works on iPhones.**
- **No signal?** Keep tapping. Check-ins are saved on your phone and sent when
  you're back online.
- **Co-hosts and club hosts** can now open the roster for events they help run.

**At the end**
- Once the event has started, **"Mark the other N as no-show"** appears under
  the roster. Only press it when you know who didn't come. It marks everyone
  not checked in, never you or your co-hosts. You get 10 seconds to undo, and
  anyone who turns up late can still be checked in.
- **The morning after is yours.** At 10:00 the day after, you get a list of
  everyone who wasn't checked in. Until midnight that day you can check in
  anyone who came, or tap **Excuse** for someone who had a reason (cancelled
  on WhatsApp, an emergency). At midnight, anyone still unmarked counts as a
  no-show. This only happens if you checked in at least half the room; if
  you didn't run check-in, nobody is marked.
- **Mistakes after that** go through the member: they tap "I was there", and
  a moderator asks you what happened. You're never the one who decides a
  dispute about your own event.

**On your event form**
- **Seat commitment:** *Automatic* (an event with limited spots counts as
  limited), *Limited* (a headcount promised to a venue, even without a spot
  cap), or *Open* (nobody loses a seat if someone doesn't come). You can't change
  it once the event has started.

**In your approval queue**
- A 🟨 or 🟥 badge next to a request tells you the member currently has a card.
  A member with a red card has to be approved by you for a seat at a limited
  event; that's why their request is in your queue.

**What it asks of you**
1. Check people in as they arrive, every time.
2. At the end, mark the rest as no-show, but only if you're sure.
3. Look at the list the morning after, and excuse anyone who had a reason, by midnight.

Questions, or something that looks wrong: message Nate.
