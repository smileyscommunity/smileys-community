# GEORGIA — Visas & staying legal
> HIGHEST STAKES: people act on this to cross a border and stay legal.

**Title:** Visas, the One-Year Rule and Staying Legal in Georgia
**Scope:** country: GE

## Article text

## One year, no visa — for most of usSince 1 January 2026 every tourist entering Georgia must carry valid health and accident insurance covering the whole stay, with cover of at least GEL 30,000. Paper or screen, Georgian or English, a foreign insurer is fine, and holders of a Georgian residence permit are exempt — but without it you can be refused at the border, so buy it before you fly. With that done, Georgia runs one of the loosest entry regimes in the region. Government Ordinance No. 255 of 5 June 2015, still amended as recently as February 2026, lists the countries whose citizens enter without a visa, and for the great majority — 94 countries and territories — the allowance is 'one full year' from each entry. A handful get less: 90 days for Chile, Fiji, North Macedonia, Paraguay, Peru and Uruguay, 45 for Iran, 30 for China, Hong Kong and Macau. Check your own passport on geoconsul.gov.ge rather than trusting a count. The list covers every EU state, the UK, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland, the USA, Canada, Mexico, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Thailand, Israel, the Gulf states, Türkiye, Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Armenia, Kazakhstan and the Central Asian republics. A separate rule lets holders of a valid visa or residence permit from the EU/EFTA, the USA, the UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Israel, New Zealand or a Gulf state enter for up to 90 days in any 180, so long as that document is valid on arrival.
Ordinary passport holders from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Vietnam and Egypt need a visa; Chinese citizens get 30 days visa-free. Short-stay applicants use the e-visa portal (evisa.gov.ge). The tourism board says a decision on a visa 'is made within 5 to 30 calendar days, depending on the visa category', so don't book flights first.

## The visa run — and why it is no longer a sure thingBecause the year runs from each entry, people have long treated Georgia as a place you can live indefinitely by crossing to Armenia or Türkiye once a year and coming straight back. That still works for most people. It has not been guaranteed since 2022. Border police can refuse entry citing 'other cases envisaged by Georgian legislation' with no further explanation, and the refusals have grown: JAMnews has reported Russians increasingly deported or turned back when trying to reset their stay; the Committee to Protect Journalists counts at least sixteen foreign journalists denied entry since 2022, six of them European in recent months; Kazakhstan has asked Tbilisi to explain refusals of its citizens, and France and Poland issued travel advisories in May 2025. Unpaid fines, a political profile or a passport full of Georgian stamps and no visible income can all be enough.

> The overstay trap. Under amendments passed in June 2025 and in force from autumn 2025, overstaying is no longer a fine you pay on the way out. Up to three months over brings an entry ban of six months; three months to a year, two years; over a year, three years — each with a fine that scales, and an unpaid fine blocks any future visa or entry. A register of illegally present foreigners went live on 1 October 2025, and appealing a refusal or deportation no longer suspends it. Count your days from the entry stamp, not from memory.

## When you want more than a stamp: the residence permitA temporary residence permit is what turns you from a tourist into a resident: it gives you a residence card with an 11-digit personal number, the key to banking, a registered lease, an Individual Entrepreneur registration and most online government services. The main routes on the Public Service Development Agency's list: work (employed or running a business; income at least five times the subsistence minimum, and from 1 March 2026 a formal work authorisation must come first, with a transition period to 1 January 2027); study at an accredited institution; family reunification with a permit holder; short-term/property — title to non-agricultural real estate worth at least USD 150,000 by accredited valuation, raised from USD 100,000 on 1 March 2026, with the old figure kept for anyone whose ownership was registered before that date, even if they apply later; investment from USD 300,000 with turnover conditions; and a new IT permit (two years' experience, USD 25,000 annual income, three-year validity renewable to twelve). Check every threshold at sda.gov.ge before you buy or sign anything — three of them changed in 2025–26.
Apply while you are still legally in the country — the agency asks for the application 40 days before your lawful stay runs out — at any Public Service Hall (the 'Justice House' in Tbilisi and the regional cities), a territorial office, or online at services.sda.gov.ge. Standard processing is 30 days, with paid 20- and 10-day options. Police clearance certificates from home are increasingly requested. Once the permit is granted, collect the residence card, then register your address within one month — it is free, but needs the property owner's written consent.

## The right to work, new since March 2026This is the change that catches people out, because it undoes the thing Georgia was famous for. A government resolution adopted on 20 February 2026 and in force from 1 March 2026 created a work-authorisation regime where none had existed: being in the country lawfully — on the visa-free year, on any stamp — no longer carries the right to work or to run a business. The right to work is now a separate permission, granted through the State Employment Support Agency, and it applies whether you are on a local payroll, doing paid work here, working remotely for a Georgian employer, or self-employed.
It arrived in stages: employees from 1 March 2026, the self-employed — freelancers and Individual Entrepreneurs included — from 1 May 2026, and anyone already registered as a labour immigrant and active on 1 March has until 1 January 2027. That last date is the one most of us are actually under, and it is close. Working without the authorisation carries a GEL 2,000 fine that can land on the employer, the employee or the self-employed person alike; a second offence doubles it, a third triples it.
It also reorders the nomad playbook. The 1% Small Business Status below is no longer the first move: a foreign national needs the authorisation active before earning the revenue the 1% is meant to tax, so the old sequence — register the Individual Entrepreneur, start invoicing, sort the paperwork afterwards — is now backwards, and the wrong order is what the fine is for. Two changes from March 2026 are worth knowing before you file: Small Business Status now takes effect on the date you request it rather than the first of the following month, and a monthly declaration is compulsory even in a month with no turnover — a missed filing is an offence now, where it used to pass as a zero return. One case the sources do not settle is the member employed by a foreign company who simply lives here: the rule is written around local employers and the self-employed, and that gap is worth an hour of an accountant's time rather than an assumption in either direction.

## The tax line at 183 daysSpend 183 days or more in Georgia in any continuous twelve-month period ending in the tax year and you are a Georgian tax resident. Georgia taxes individuals territorially — genuinely foreign-source income (dividends, interest, gains on assets abroad) is generally untaxed — but 'foreign' is about where the work happens, not where the money lands: a freelancer sitting in Vake billing a client in Berlin is earning Georgian-source income. That is why nomads register as an Individual Entrepreneur with Small Business Status at the Revenue Service (rs.ge): 1% of turnover up to GEL 500,000 a year, 3% on anything above, monthly declarations, and the status lapses after two years over the cap. Lawyers, doctors, architects, auditors and consultants are excluded. Since 2026 there is a gate in front of it: the work authorisation above has to be active first, and the status now takes effect from the day you file for it rather than later. Take the 2026 rules to an accountant — the rs.ge pages still carry older figures.

## The neighbours who arrived in 2022Say it plainly: since February 2022 Georgia has absorbed a large Russian-speaking arrival. The 2024 census counted 37,715 Russian citizens among 133,857 foreign citizens, alongside 11,542 Ukrainians (1,631 in 2014) and 4,473 Belarusians (101); ethnic Russians numbered 42,525, up from 26,453 in 2014; The Insider puts the three groups at about 108,000 by 2026. Tbilisi rents doubled year-on-year in May 2022. Many Georgians hold the Russian state, not its émigrés, responsible for the occupied 20% of their country — but the welcome is cooler than the visa regime suggests, and the border refusals above fall hardest on exactly this group.

# GEORGIA — Bank accounts

**Title:** Opening a Bank Account in Georgia: Cards, Cash, the Lari and the Compliance Desk
**Scope:** country: GE

## Article text

## Three banks that matterGeorgian retail banking is a duopoly with a third wheel. Bank of Georgia (orange) and TBC (blue) hold roughly three-quarters of the system between them; Liberty Bank (green) has the widest branch network in the regions and a reputation for being easier on foreigners. All three open multi-currency accounts — one card, separate GEL, USD and EUR balances (Liberty adds GBP), and TBC lets you 'open additional accounts in any other currency of your choice'. Apple Pay has worked here since September 2019 at all three; Google Pay is supported through the TBC and Bank of Georgia apps.

## Opening one as a foreignerOn paper it is easy. TBC's expat page says 'you only need a foreign passport and a phone number', and the account 'is opened instantly', with a digital card at once. The phone number must be Georgian — SMS codes will not go to a foreign number — so buy the SIM first. In the branch you will be handed what TBC calls a self-declaration 'on the goal of opening an account and expected banking transactions', and the bank 'may additionally request documents evidencing the source of origin of funds'. This is the compliance questionnaire every foreigner now gets: what do you do, where does the money come from, why Georgia, how much will move through the account each month. Answer consistently and bring a home-bank statement, a lease and proof of income. Since Georgia aligned with FATF rules the National Bank requires each client to be risk-scored, and a decision can take up to two weeks. Citizens of sanctioned countries — Syria, Iran, Afghanistan, North Korea — are refused by default. A residence card with a personal number shortens all of this.
Russian citizens have had a harder time, though the sharpest of this is from 2022 and 2023 and members report the picture varies by branch and by year. In April 2022 OC Media reported at least one Bank of Georgia branch requiring Russians to sign a form recognising Russia as 'an aggressor' and 'occupant' of Georgia and Ukraine; from December 2022 all Georgian banks warned clients that accounts used to breach Western sanctions would be blocked without notice, and in April 2023 TBC said it 'reserves the right to close customer accounts without prior notice' if it identifies a sanctions-violating transaction. Refusals for Russian and Belarusian passports are reported but not universal — expect more questions, not an automatic no.

## Cards, ATMs and the lariContactless is everywhere, down to the Tbilisi metro and buses, which take a tapped bank card directly. ATMs from the big three are dense in the cities and dispense lari; many Bank of Georgia and TBC machines also give dollars from your USD balance. Withdrawing from your own bank is free or nearly so; a foreign card pays a small fee per withdrawal plus whatever your home bank charges.
The lari (GEL, ₾) floats, and it moves. It hit a record low of 3.44 to the dollar in the pandemic, fell to 3.38 in the two weeks after the February 2022 invasion, then rallied to 2.72 by August 2022 on the inflows that came with it. It has been steadier since, around 2.6 to the dollar in the autumn of 2026. The National Bank publishes an official rate each day at nbg.gov.ge, but it is indicative; the yellow exchange booths on every street set their own and are usually within a whisker of it. The economy is heavily dollarised: landlords quote and often collect rent in USD, and savings sit in dollars. Keep your salary currency in its own balance and convert as you spend rather than in one lump.

## Where cash still rulesBazaars (Dezerter, Navtlugi, Lilo), the neighbourhood fruit stall, most marshrutka minibuses out of town, village guesthouses and the funicular want notes. Keep a couple of hundred lari on you outside the centre.

## Getting money inSWIFT to your USD or EUR balance works from any bank and is how most members are paid. Wise sends to Georgian bank accounts in GEL — 'the recipient gets money in GEL directly from Wise's local bank account' — and the usual remittance systems pay out here. A Georgian resident cannot get the Wise card — it is not offered here — so open the account before you move and keep it on your old address. Revolut does not onboard Georgian residents at all as of late 2025, though a Revolut card from home works fine in shops and ATMs.

## Paying the rent and the billsUtilities are tied to a subscriber number (abonenti) on the flat, not to you — ask the landlord for the electricity (Telasi in Tbilisi, Energo-Pro elsewhere), gas (Tbilisi Energy) and water (Georgian Water &amp; Power) numbers on day one. Type them into the Payments section of the bank app and the balance appears; pay in seconds. The same works at the paybox kiosks on every block — blue for TBC, orange for Bank of Georgia, other colours for independents — which take cash for a small commission. Rent goes by bank transfer to the landlord's account or, very commonly, in cash dollars against a signed receipt; a registered lease helps you both.

> The quiet-closure trap. A Georgian account is not a set-and-forget thing. Banks re-run their compliance checks — annually, or whenever a transfer looks unusual, and crypto exchanges are a reliable trigger — and send an SMS or email asking for updated documents. The requests are time-limited: miss the window and the account is frozen until you turn up at a branch, sometimes with a fresh source-of-funds file. Accounts that sit idle with no transactions can be closed under the bank's standard terms, and the period is set in your own contract rather than by law — find it there, because no two banks quote the same number. Keep the Georgian SIM alive — the bank talks to you through it — and log in once a month even when you are away.

# GEORGIA — SIM & internet

**Title:** SIM Cards, eSIM and Home Internet in Georgia: Magti, Silknet, Cellfie
**Scope:** country: GE

## Article text

## Three networks, two of them renamedGeorgia has three mobile operators and you will meet all three at the airport. Magti (Magticom, red) is the biggest and has the coverage that reaches into the mountains. Silknet (purple) swallowed the old Geocell network in 2018; the Geocell name still lives in its web addresses and in what older shopkeepers call it. Cellfie (yellow, 23%) is the former Beeline Georgia: VEON sold it to a local buyer in June 2022 and it took the Cellfie name in 2023 to shed the Russian association. Any 'Beeline' or 'Geocell' SIM you are handed is one of these two. All three run 4G everywhere that matters and 5G in the cities.

## Buying a SIM: passport, no exceptionsGeorgian law requires every SIM to be registered to an identity document, so a physical passport (or a residence card) is scanned and tied to the number at the counter — a photo on your phone is refused. Buy at an operator shop, at the counters in the arrivals hall, or at the many corner-shop dealers with the operator's sticker; the price is the same everywhere and there is no airport mark-up. The SIM itself costs little and comes with a small starting balance; you then buy a monthly package in the app. Tell the clerk you want a standard number if you are staying, not the tourist one — the difference matters below.

## eSIMAll three sell eSIMs. Magti issues one for a new or existing number through the MyMagti app or any service centre, but only after you have passed its identification step, which for a foreigner in practice means a visit with the passport. Silknet lets you choose a number and buy an eSIM on its website, and Cellfie's app offers 'Buy eSIM' or 'Switch SIM card to eSIM'. If you just want data for a week, the global travel eSIMs (Airalo and the like) ride on these same networks at a higher price per gigabyte and skip the counter; if you are moving here, get a real number — the bank, the tax portal and every delivery app will want it.

## Tourist packages vs a resident numberEach operator sells a tourist bundle — Magti's airport 'welcome package' at Tbilisi and Kutaisi is a free SIM with a 15-day allowance of data, local calls and some international minutes; Silknet's are branded Tourist Start and Tourist Unlimited. They are excellent for a fortnight and useless after it: the packages are fixed-term, the number is meant to be thrown away, and the balance cannot be turned into a normal plan. A standard prepaid number with a monthly package is what residents use; unlimited data on Magti or Silknet is cheap by Istanbul standards, and postpaid contracts exist for residence-card holders. Ask for what you want in gigabytes and minutes rather than by name: all three rename their packages yearly, and last year's name gets you a blank look.

## Your phone: no registration, unlike TürkiyeMembers arriving from Türkiye will ask first. We found no IMEI or device-registration scheme in Georgia: nothing on the Communications Commission's site, no blocked-phone deadline, and any unlocked handset from anywhere simply works on a Georgian SIM indefinitely. One travel-eSIM blog claims registration is needed after 30 days; there is no Georgian source for it, Georgia appears on none of the IMEI-registration country lists, and we treat it as a confusion with Türkiye or Azerbaijan.

> The dead-number trap. A prepaid number that goes unpaid and unused is recycled after a period of inactivity. Magti at least publishes the answer and the remedy: its Number Maintenance Service insures a number against termination for 180 days for GEL 1, switched on with *180# or in MyMagti and renewed on the 181st day — with the sting that if a frozen number's balance falls below that GEL 1 the fee cannot be taken and the number goes anyway. Silknet and Cellfie run comparable schemes; ask at the counter for yours while the number still works. Your bank, the Revenue Service, the residence-permit portal and your two-factor codes are all tied to that number, so losing it while you are abroad locks you out of everything at once. Keep a small package running while you travel, or switch the number to a monthly plan that renews from your card.

## Home internetFixed broadband is essentially two companies: Magti (about 47% of fixed lines) and Silknet (about 31%), both mostly fibre-to-the-building in Tbilisi, Batumi, Kutaisi and Rustavi, with Silknet also selling DSL and 4.5G wireless boxes where fibre has not arrived. Before you sign a lease, ask which of the two is wired into the building — in older blocks in Sololaki, the Old Town and parts of Saburtalo it is often only one, and the answer decides your provider. Installation is usually a few days, needs the landlord's consent and someone at home, and bundles with the same company's mobile and TV are discounted. Speeds of 100–300 Mbps are normal in the centre. Starlink has been licensed since November 2023 for villages the fibre never reached.

## Outside TbilisiCoverage is good along the roads and in every town. In Svaneti, Tusheti, Khevsureti and the high passes only Magti reliably holds a signal; Silknet is close behind; Cellfie is a city network and should not be your only SIM for a road trip. Download offline maps before you leave the plain.

## The airport countersAt Tbilisi all three operators have desks in the arrivals hall, staffed whenever flights are landing, day or night. Kutaisi has Magti and Silknet on the Wizz Air schedule. Batumi's arrivals hall has operator counters too, though the hours are shorter and a late landing can find them shut. Queues form when two flights arrive together; if you have an eSIM-capable phone, the airport Wi-Fi and an online eSIM get you moving faster.

## The legal noteGeorgia does not block social networks or messaging apps and has no law against VPNs; Freedom House counts a few hundred sites blocked since 2017, nearly all for copyright, and notes that operators must install lawful-intercept equipment under the communications law — the same as most of Europe. Use whatever you used at home.

# TBILISI — Getting around (Metromoney)

**Title:** Metromoney: One Card for the Metro, Buses, Minibuses and Cable Cars
**Scope:** city: tbilisi

## Article text

## One company, one cardAlmost everything municipal in Tbilisi belongs to the Tbilisi Transport Company (TTC), wholly owned by City Hall: the metro since 1966, the city buses since 2009, the Rike–Narikala cable car since 2012, the parking since 2018. Head office Station Square No. 2; hotline 032 293 44 44. The card is the Metromoney: anonymous, no ID needed, valid on the metro, the buses, the city minibuses and three cable cars. Buy it at any metro station payment office (the salaro window) or at the Rike–Narikala ticket offices; TTC also lists Bank of Georgia express branches. Top up at any metro station, at both Rike–Narikala stations, and at Bank of Georgia express-pay machines (the orange-and-white boxes, cash) and service centres. Bank of Georgia is the only bank that loads it — TTC's own page names no other, and the maintained city guides agree. The balance shows on the metro gate as you pass and on the bus ticket.

> The receipt trap. TTC restores a lost or damaged Metromoney only against the receipt printed when you bought it — no receipt, no transfer of balance, and the card's refund within a month needs it too. Photograph it. The 'problematic card' desks are inside Avlabari and Medical University stations.

## The 90-minute ruleTTC's standard tariff is a time window, not a ride: one tap buys 90 minutes of unlimited travel across the metro, the buses, the minibuses and the Bagebi–University ropeway — metro, bus, metro again on a single fare. Day, week, month and longer passes exist, but only on a personalised subscription card from a metro payment office. TTC's page extends the package to 'different bank cards', so a contactless Visa, Mastercard or phone boards everything; a foreign card is charged about 1.50 GEL a tap against Metromoney's 1 GEL, and the guides still disagree on whether it earns the transfers. Buy a Metromoney at the first window you pass and the question stops mattering. Two things definitely get no window: cash (still taken on some minibuses) and payment through the mobile app.

## The metro: two lines, one interchangeLine 1, the Akhmeteli–Varketili line, runs 16 stations from Akhmetelis Teatri in the north to Varketili in the south-east; Line 2, the Saburtalo line, runs seven from Station Square out to State University (opened 2017). Station Square (Sadguris Moedani), under the central railway station, is the only interchange. Trains run from a little before 06:00 until about midnight, the last one leaving each terminus around 23:50 — so a station mid-line sees it earlier in one direction and later in the other. New CRRC trains ordered in 2025 are due from 2026.

## Buses and minibusesRoute numbers are three digits: the 3xx (and 2xx) routes are TTC's full-size buses — blue MAN and green Isuzu, with 200 gas-powered 18-metre MANs entering service from 27 September 2025 — and the 4xx/5xx routes are minibuses run by the separate Tbilisi Minibus company (tm.ge). The old yellow marshrutkas were withdrawn in 2021–22 and replaced by blue-grey minibuses on fixed numbered routes; they still stop on request and still show the destination in Georgian. Metromoney and bank cards work on both. Rustaveli Avenue is closed to all traffic from 23 July to the end of November 2026 for rebuilding, between Tamar Chovelidze Street and Freedom Square: some twenty bus routes, 337 among them, are diverted via Heroes' Square and the embankment, and several minibuses no longer reach Rustaveli metro.

## Live trackingTTC's own app is Tbilisi Transport Company (iOS and Android, linked from ttc.com.ge): planner, live arrivals by stop, offline timetables, with the same data on the web at transit.ttc.com.ge. The 'Tbilisi Bus' app in the stores is third-party; Google Maps carries the live feed too.

## Cable cars and the funicularRike–Narikala (2012), Rike Park to the fortress, is TTC's, charges its own single fare and takes Metromoney only — its windows sell the card. TTC publishes no hours; the maintained guides put it at 10:00 to midnight in summer and 10:00 to 22:00 in winter, on its own 2.5 GEL fare. Turtle Lake (Kus Tba), from 76 Chavchavadze Avenue beside Vake Park, reopened in 2016 with bike hooks and its own low fare; TTC took it out of service for maintenance on 23 December 2025 and has published nothing since, while the travel guides go on listing it at 10:00 to 22:00 — either a stale page or a quiet resumption. Ask at the lower station before you build a morning around it. The Bagebi–University ropeway, from the university's Maghlivi building across the Vere valley, is municipal transport inside the 90-minute window. The Mtatsminda funicular, from Daniel Chonkadze Street up to the park, is the park's, not TTC's: Metromoney is not valid; buy the park's own card at either station.

## The airportThe airport's own transport pages list two things: bus 337 — stop in front of Arrivals, roughly 07:00 to 23:00, via Kakheti Highway, Avlabari and Freedom Square to Station Square, roughly every 15–20 minutes, Metromoney or bank card, diverted off Rustaveli until December — and the taxi rank. The railway station 70 metres from the terminal is no longer listed: guides updated in August 2026 say no trains are running, with a resumption 'later this year', and nothing had changed by September. Check gr.com.ge before you plan around it.

> The airport taxi trap. The official rank is at the kerb outside Arrivals, 24 hours; expect a fixed zone price rather than a meter, and reckon on several times the app fare into town. Anyone who approaches you inside the terminal is not it — the reported fares are several times the app price. Order a Bolt instead; the terminal Wi-Fi is unreliable for apps, so use your own data.

## Taxis in townBolt is the default — Estonian, card-in-app, Economy to XL plus an Electric option. Yandex Go is often cheaper but is Russian-owned: in September 2024 Georgia's Personal Data Protection Service fined its local operator for sending users' and drivers' IP addresses to servers in Russia, and many Georgians boycott it on principle. Choose knowingly. Maxim is the third app. Sit in the back.

## Leaving townThree stations, by direction. Didube (Okriba), 153 Tsereteli Avenue at Didube metro: everything north and west — Mtskheta, Gudauri, Kazbegi, Gori, Borjomi, Kutaisi, Batumi. Navtlughi beside Samgori metro: Kakheti — Sighnaghi, Telavi. Ortachala, the Central Bus Station at 5 Dimitri Gulia Street, has no metro and now mostly handles international coaches (Yerevan, Baku, Turkey), ticketed on tkt.ge. A ban on minibuses over 150 km and between the big cities was announced for 1 June 2025; it was deferred to 2027, and the vans are running. Trains leave Tbilisi Central above Station Square: Stadler double-deckers to Batumi via Kutaisi Airport, sold out days ahead in summer — book on gr.com.ge or tkt.ge — the daily Baku train, back since 26 May 2026, and Yerevan.

## On foot and behind the wheelThe old town, Sololaki and Mtatsminda are staircases as much as streets, and the metro leaves you at the bottom of them; a Bolt up and a walk down is the local pattern. Driving is fast and improvised, and central parking is TTC's zonal-hour system, paid through the Tbilisi Parking app. Most newcomers never buy a car.
